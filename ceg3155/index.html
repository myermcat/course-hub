The Submission column is filled in by hand: each cell is a named slot, ceg-lab-1-submission through ceg-lab-4-submission, and the link component on this page puts the Google Doc address saved under that key into the cell.

Laboratory 1 is marked against the restrictions in section 6 of its handout. The design is VHDL at the structural level of modelling, and both behavioural modelling and Verilog are refused. No IP core may be instantiated, from the Vivado catalogue or from anywhere else, so every atomic block down to the D flip-flop is built by the group. The circuit has to be synchronous and globally resettable, which means a clock signal and a reset signal in every functional block. Every register runs on the board's single 100 MHz oscillator and advances on a one-cycle enable pulse at a divide ratio of 100,000,000, which gives a step about once a second; a divided clock routed to the registers loses marks. A working version is demonstrated to the teaching assistant before the report is due.

What carries the marks

The midterm cannot be transferred to the final. A missed midterm is a lost 20 percent, so the date is worth protecting. It falls the evening after the Signal and System Analysis midterm.

Three quizzes run and the best two count, 2.5 percent each, thirty minutes at the start of a Thursday lecture. The laboratories carry 15 percent across four of them, three hours a week in CBY B402. Quizzes, the midterm and the final are all written in person.

The toolchain, and what runs on the Mac

The laboratories and the project are VHDL on hardware, so where the work happens is decided before any of it starts.

The board is the Digilent Nexys A7-100T, carrying an Artix-7 XC7A100T-1CSG324C, and the software is Xilinx Vivado. The Lab 1 handout names both, and the pin assignments in its Table 2 are Vivado XDC constraints. The course website describes an older Altera DE-2 flow under Quartus II, and the handout and the Brightspace appendices replace that text.

Vivado runs on Windows and Linux x86. The macOS route Prof. Abielmona posted is a virtual machine, VMware Fusion with Windows 11 ARM, wanting 16 GB of memory and 250 GB of free disk, and programming the board over JTAG does not work through it on Apple Silicon. The M1 MacBook has 8 GB of memory and about 42 GB free, so synthesis, the bitstream and programming the board happen on a laboratory machine in CBY B402, on the project partner's machine, or over the remote connection to a laboratory machine covered in part 0D of the install guide.

On the Mac, nvc and surfer run natively on Apple Silicon and are installed. They cover writing VHDL, compiling it, running a testbench and reading the waveform. There is no synthesis and no board behind them. The Lab 1 scaffold is in lab1 in the course folder, where make sim runs the testbench and make wave opens the result in surfer.

What the course covers

  • Finite state machines of the Mealy and Moore kinds, with state tables and state diagrams
  • State reduction and state assignment, and sequential circuits that are incompletely specified
  • Implementation with D and JK flip-flops
  • ASM charts, the algorithmic state machine notation for describing a controller
  • VHDL, the hardware description language every laboratory is written in
  • Synchronous design with programmable logic devices, ROM and FPGA
  • Computer interfaces and controllers
  • Asynchronous circuits and hazards
  • SystemC
