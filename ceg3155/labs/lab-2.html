The date is the Wednesday one. The course runs a Monday laboratory group and a Wednesday one, and a laboratory is due at midnight on the day its group meets. Yours is the Wednesday group.

The board and the software belong to the course. Both are named in the Laboratory 1 handout and run through the term, and whether Laboratory 2 needs the board at all is answered by its own handout.

1. The gist

What the circuit has to do, described the way you would describe it to somebody who will never open the handout: what the board shows, what the switches do, and how the display behaves as you change them.

2. The kit

The board, the software, the room, the evening, and how many people are in a group. Laboratory 1 ran on the Nexys A7-100T under Vivado 2022.1 in CBY B402, and this handout says whether any of that changes.

3. What is happening inside the circuit

The registers the design holds, what each one carries, and the two or three decisions that settle whether the circuit works at all.

4. What the reset does

Which flip flops the reset forces, the value each one takes, and what the demonstration to the teaching assistant opens with.

5. What you are actually graded on

The stages of the design method that carry the marks. Laboratory 1 wanted five of them, from pseudocode through to the control logic, and the VHDL came last.

6. The rules that cost marks

The restrictions the marker applies. Laboratory 1 refused Verilog, refused behavioural modelling and refused ready made blocks, and it wanted a synchronous, globally resettable design stepped by an enable pulse.

7. How to approach it

What to do in the first session, what to agree before the building is split between two people, and what to leave until the two halves come back together.

8. What goes in the report

The handout's own list of what the report has to contain, and the parts of it worth planning for while the work is still going on.

What to do in the meantime

Read the Laboratory 1 guide. The design method the marks are for, the restrictions on the VHDL and the shape of the report are set out there, and the marking in this course has followed the same pattern so far. Read it as the pattern to expect, and take this laboratory's own requirements from its handout once it appears.

Settle the machine that runs Vivado before this laboratory starts. Synthesis and programming the board need Windows or Linux on x86, and an install started on the laboratory evening costs most of that evening.

Open the module on Brightspace in the week before the laboratory. The other place a handout could appear is the labs folder of the course site, which has answered 403 Forbidden since term started.
